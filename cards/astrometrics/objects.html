Objects ​

objects is the list of what the scene draws: planets, grids, routes, nebulas, ships. Each object has a type, and its other keys depend on the type.

  • Pick a type in Which type for what; each links to its keys on one of the four pages below.
  • Positions, sizes and distances are in scene units, set by the card's units (Scale). Line widths and dashes are in px.
PageTypes
Grids, Spheres and Volumesgrid, volume, sphere, ring, image
Lines and Lightpath, beam, trail, lightning, glow
Space Phenomenanebula, belt, asteroid, comet, galaxy, crystalline
Ships and Stationsmodel, cube

Real cards with sample entities. Drag to turn a tile, scroll or pinch to zoom.

Which type for what ​

The type to use for each thing you might want in the scene. Each name links to its keys.

A wireframe: a sector map, a galaxy disc, a sky dome, a see-through ball, a tube, a gravity wellgrid

An orbit, a range ring, a rimring
A route through points, an arched route, the sun's trackpath
A weapon, transporter or sensor beam (a glowing straight line)beam
Where a moving point has been (a fading line behind a ship or planet)trail
A star, a galactic core, a beacon (a soft light)glow
Clouds of gasnebula
An asteroid belt (a band of rocks round a centre)belt

A spiral galaxy (stars, arms, a bar and dust; the Milky Way by default)galaxy

Keys every object takes ​

These keys work on every type: where the object sits, how it is turned, when it shows and what a tap does.

  • Colours take theme tokens (theme:colors.ui.primary), CSS variables (var(--lcars-blue)) or plain colours.
  • Under reduced motion, moving parts (spin, pulses, drifting gas, strikes) stop; each type says what it shows instead. Moving effects are on the Effects page.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 280
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 20
      tilt: 30
      distance: 110
points:
  - id: planet
    xyz: [25, 0, 0]
    label: PLANET
    style: minor
objects:
  - type: sphere
    at: planet
    radius: 6
  # sits at a point, tipped over and spinning
  - type: ring
    id: rings
    at: planet
    radius: 14
    rotation: [30, 0, 0]
    spin: 30
    color: var(--lcars-orange)
  # half see-through, with a tag for rules
  - type: grid
    shape: sphere
    at: [-25, 0, 0]
    radius: 15
    opacity: 0.5
    tags: [survey]
More about keys every object takes
  • Looks: an object without look shows in every look, and one with a diagram form switches its parts with the look: a mapped sphere, a filled grid, a glow, cube, asteroid, belt, comet, galaxy, crystalline entity or volume nebula shows its wireframe in the diagram look, and a model with its own materials shows as a hologram. A ring drawn as a line, and a path, hide in the realistic look. look: all shows it in every look, as configured.
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
idstringtype#nA name for points standing on it (object), rules and fly-to. The first grid is grid#1

rotation[x, y, z][0, 0, 0]Turns it, degrees: y is a bearing, x tips it toward the camera, z rolls it

Facing the way it goes ​

face: motion turns an object to face where its point is going: a ship rides its orbit nose first.

  • The object needs at: a moving point. Without face, the object slides along keeping its own turn.
  • The bow is +z on every built-in model. A model file with its bow along +x needs rotation: [0, 90, 0].
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 320
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 20
      tilt: 35
      distance: 120
points:
  - id: ship
    polar: [40, 90]
    object: orbit
    label: ENTERPRISE
    style: tiny
objects:
  - type: ring
    id: orbit
    radius: 40
    spin: 12
    width: 0.5
    opacity: 0.4
  - type: model
    at: ship
    src: builtin:constitution_class_refit
    size: 8
    face: motion
  - type: trail
    point: ship
    length: 4
    width: 2
More about facing the way it goes
  • Level flight: the object's top stays up, so a ship stays level. rotation applies on top, in the object's own frame.
  • Where the heading comes from: the point's movement however it moves (a spinning ring, an entity, a rule), measured on the object the point stands on. A ship on a ring round a planet that is itself on an orbit faces round the planet, not along the planet's way.
  • Before it moves: before the point first moves, and under reduced motion (which holds spin), a point on a spinning object faces along the spin. Anything else keeps its rotation until the point moves.
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
facestring-motion: turn the object's +z toward where its point is going, top up

Facing a point ​

face: <point id> keeps an object turned toward a point, wherever it is (another star system, across the galaxy), as either moves.

  • Most objects turn their +z to the point, top up. Objects round an axis (a grid other than a plane, a ring, a belt, a galaxy, a whirlpool nebula) point their axis at it instead, so a ring lies face-on to it.
  • A well's narrow end leads to the point it faces, and a wormhole is two wells facing each other.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 300
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 20
      tilt: 30
      distance: 130
points:
  - id: beacon
    polar: [45, 0]
    object: track
    marker: reticle
    label: BEACON
objects:
  - type: ring
    id: track
    radius: 45
    spin: 20
    opacity: 0.3
  # turns its +z (the bow) to the beacon
  - type: model
    src: builtin:constitution_class_refit
    size: 30
    face: beacon
  # turns its axis to the beacon, so it lies face-on to it
  - type: ring
    at: [0, 22, 0]
    radius: 8
    face: beacon
    color: var(--lcars-blue)
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
facestring-A point id: turn toward that point. rotation applies on top

Distance ​

Show an object only within a range of camera distances: planets that appear as you zoom in. Zoom into the star below: its planet and orbit appear as you close in, and the disc gives way.

  • The distance is measured to the object's nearest edge, so a large grid does not vanish while you fly through it.
  • To swap a model for a marker far away, give the model max_distance: 60 and give a point with marker: ship (or starbase) min_distance: 60. Touching ranges cross-fade, so one stands in for the other.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 320
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 20
      tilt: 35
      distance: 420
points:
  - id: star
    xyz: [0, 0, 0]
    marker: dot
    label: SOL
    style: major
    min_distance: 140
  - id: earth
    polar: [30, 60]
    object: orbit
    label: EARTH
    style: minor
    max_distance: 150
objects:
  - type: glow
    size: 24
    color: var(--lcars-yellow)
  - type: ring
    id: orbit
    radius: 30
    color: var(--lcars-blue)
    spin: 6
    max_distance: 120
  - type: sphere
    at: earth
    radius: 2.5
    image: builtin:earth_blue_marble
    line_width: 0
    max_distance: 120
  # gives way as you close in
  - type: grid
    shape: disc
    radius: 90
    line_spacing: 30
    opacity: 0.6
    min_distance: 120
More about distance
  • Points: the keys are the same as on points.
  • Inside and above: the distance is 0 when the camera is inside the object. A flat grid (shape: plane or disc, not deformed) is measured to its surface: above a galactic disc the distance is your height over it, so min_distance can hide the disc once you dive into a star system.
  • Out of range: an object out of range is not drawn and cannot be tapped; its points keep their own ranges.
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
max_distancenumber-Hidden when the camera is farther than this. It fades out over the next 10% of that distance
min_distancenumber-Hidden when the camera is closer than this. It fades in over the 10% of that distance before it
fade[start, end]-Fully shown nearer than start, gone past end
