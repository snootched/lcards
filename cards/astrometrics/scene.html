Scene and Looks ​

The settings for the whole scene: its units and motion, how it looks, what is behind it and how its labels are drawn. What the scene holds is on the Objects and Points pages; the camera is on Camera and Views.

Units and motion ​

Scene units are yours to pick, and every moving key works in the same units: degrees or scene units per second.

  • Sizes, distances and positions are in scene units: a galaxy disc of radius 100, a planet of radius 10. Line widths, dashes, label sizes and card offsets are in px, so they hold their size at any zoom.
  • For real data, name the units with units (km, au, ly ...): a solar system in km, a galaxy in light years with Sol inside it in km. The card holds the whole range, from a galaxy down to a station 1.5 km across. See Scale.
  • Motion is off until you set it. Turning is in degrees per second (spin, camera.auto_rotate); travel is in its own units per second (dash.speed in px, a fill's speed and a nebula's or noise's drift in scene units); repeating cycles are a period in seconds (scan.period, a deformation's period). Fly-to times are in ms, as everywhere in LCARdS.
  • time_scale runs all that motion slower or faster. The sky, the scene clock and the camera are not affected.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 280
# everything turns at half the speed it is set to
time_scale: 0.5
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 20
      tilt: 25
      distance: 130
objects:
  # 20 degrees a second, so 10 at this time scale
  - type: sphere
    radius: 25
    spin: 20
    line_spacing_deg: 15
    color: var(--lcars-blue)
  - type: ring
    radius: 40
    spin: -30
    dash: [6, 8]
    color: var(--lcars-orange)
More about motion
  • Bearings are degrees clockwise from north (north is away from the default camera), as on a compass seen from above.
  • On screen: the time slider under the camera buttons changes the speed without changing the config. A new time_scale in the config wins over it.
  • Reduced motion: spins, scans, drift and moving dashes stop whatever time_scale is (Performance and motion).
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
unitsstring-What one scene unit is: m, km, au, ly or pc. Optional. See Scale: units
time_scalenumber1Speed of the scene's motion (orbits, spins, drift, scans, trails): 0.5 half speed, 2 twice as fast, 0 holds it. Can follow an entity

Looks: diagram and realistic ​

One scene can be an LCARS diagram, a realistic view, or both. Give an object or point look: diagram or look: realistic, and the card's look picks what shows.

  • diagram shows what has look: diagram, realistic what has look: realistic, and mixed (the default) shows both. Anything without a look shows in every look, except orbits and routes (below).
  • An object without a look that has a diagram part and a realistic part switches them by itself: a mapped planet is a wire globe in the diagram look and a photograph in the realistic one, with nothing extra to write. look: all keeps an object as configured in every look.
  • Orbits and routes (a ring drawn as a line, a path) are part of the diagram: they show in the diagram and mixed looks and hide in the realistic one.
  • Switching crossfades in place, without a rebuild.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 300
look: mixed
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 20
      tilt: 20
      distance: 150
points:
  - id: name
    xyz: [0, 38, 0]
    label: MARS
    style: minor
objects:
  # a wire globe in the diagram look, the map in the realistic one
  - type: sphere
    radius: 30
    image: builtin:mars_viking
    spin: 4
    line_spacing_deg: 15
    color: var(--lcars-blue)
  # diagram only
  - type: ring
    radius: 45
    dash: [6, 8]
    look: diagram
More about looks

What each object switches when it has no look:

ObjectDiagram lookRealistic look
sphere with an image or a fill.effectIts lines over a plain body (its fill.color)The bare map or painted surface
grid with a fill.effectIts linesIts fill
model with material: originalA hologramIts own materials
glowA ring marker with four ticks, facing the cameraThe glow
cubeIts edges and a grid on each faceThe Borg cube or shaded box
asteroidA wireframe of its rockThe lit rock
beltIts inner and outer edgesThe rocks
cometIts nucleus as a wireframe, a ring round the coma, each tail as a lineNucleus, coma and tails
galaxyEach arm's spiral, the rim, a ring round the bulge and the barStars, glow and dust
crystallineIts spine and branches as lines, a ring round the coreThe crystal and its core
nebula with render: volumeIts outline: a ball's three rings, a box's edgesThe gas (and its lightning)
ring drawn as a line, pathThe lineHidden: orbits and routes are part of the diagram
  • Mixed: shows every object as configured. The wireframes (from glow down in the table) are drawn in the object's own colours and show in the diagram look only, so mixed draws those objects as configured, without the extra wireframe.
  • Rings with a band: a ring with a band image (a planet's rings) is part of the realistic picture and shows in every look.
  • Far away: an asteroid, belt or crystalline entity is a dot in every look.
  • No realistic form: for what has none, such as a wormhole's wire funnel and its glowing vortex, give each part its own look.
  • From an entity: look can follow an entity whose state (or attribute) names the look, in any case. An input_select with the options DIAGRAM, REALISTIC and MIXED, shown as a select menu next to the card, makes a quick switch. The docs have no such helper, so this example is not live:
yaml
look:
  entity: input_select.astro_look

Backdrop ​

Stars at infinity behind the scene: they turn with the camera and never move when it pans. stars: true uses the defaults.

  • Each star gets a random size between min_radius and max_radius and a random brightness within brightness; seed picks the same ones every time.
  • opacity fades the whole layer, so the stars can sit quietly behind a busy scene.
  • backdrop.dust adds depth dust: particles round the camera that stay put as it moves, so every move shows depth. It and the star streaks of a warp fly-to are on Effects: warp and dust.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 260
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 0
      tilt: 10
      distance: 150
backdrop:
  stars:
    count: 2500
    min_radius: 0.4
    max_radius: 1.4
    brightness: [0.3, 1]
    colors: [var(--lcards-moonlight), var(--lcars-blue)]
    seed: 7
objects:
  - type: glow
    at: [0, 0, 0]
    size: 40
    color: var(--lcars-yellow)
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
backdrop.starsboolean / objectoffStars at infinity. true for the defaults, or the keys below
backdrop.stars.countnumber2000How many stars, 0 to 50000
backdrop.stars.min_radiusnumber0.4Smallest star radius, px
backdrop.stars.max_radiusnumber1.2Largest star radius, px
backdrop.stars.brightness[low, high][0.35, 1]Each star's brightness is picked between these, 0 to 1
backdrop.stars.colorcolourvar(--lcards-moonlight)Star colour
backdrop.stars.colorslist-Several colours, picked at random per star. Wins over color

background_animation ​

A flat background effect behind the scene: a nebula, a warp starfield, a grid. It takes any background animation, as on the MSD card.

  • It does not turn with the camera: it is a picture behind the 3D scene. For clouds in the scene itself, use a nebula object.
  • It takes a list, so several effects can be layered behind the scene.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 280
background_animation:
  - preset: nebula
    config:
      volumetric: true
      cloud_count: 4
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 20
      tilt: 30
      distance: 150
objects:
  - type: grid
    shape: disc
    radius: 60
    pattern: polar
    line_spacing: 10
    spoke_count: 24
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
background_animationlist-Background effects behind the scene. See Background animations
background_animation[].presetstringrequiredThe effect: nebula, starfield, grid ...
background_animation[].configobjectthe preset'sThe effect's own settings

Labels ​

How every label on the card is drawn: the text keys they all start from, the label styles, overlap and pixel snapping. Point labels are LCARdS text, with the text-field keys of the other cards: fonts, sizes, weights, colours with state maps, background boxes, templates.

  • labels.default sets keys for every label. labels.styles changes the built-in styles (title, major, minor, tiny) or adds your own, which starts from minor; a point picks one with style. A style's keys win over labels.default, which wins over the built-in style.
  • When two labels overlap, the higher priority wins (the larger style), then the nearer one. overlap: true keeps them all shown; each still moves to a free side of its point when it can.
  • Labels move by sub-pixel steps, which looks smooth while the scene turns. On a weak device, snap: true rounds them to whole px.
  • What a label says, and its fields round the point, are on Points: labels.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 280
labels:
  default:
    text_transform: uppercase
    letter_spacing: 1
  styles:
    # change a built-in style
    major:
      color: var(--lcars-orange)
    # add your own
    alert:
      font_size: 16
      color: var(--lcars-red)
      priority: 90
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 0
      tilt: 40
      distance: 160
points:
  - id: home
    xyz: [0, 0, 0]
    marker: reticle
    label: Starbase
    style: major
  - id: contact
    polar: [50, 60]
    marker: triangle
    label: Unknown contact
    style: alert
  - id: buoy
    polar: [45, 220]
    marker: dot
    label: Buoy
objects:
  - type: grid
    shape: disc
    radius: 70
    pattern: polar
    line_spacing: 10
    spoke_count: 24
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
labels.defaultobject-Text keys every label on the card starts from (font_family, text_transform, letter_spacing ...), over the built-in styles
labels.stylesobject-Label styles by name: a built-in (title, major, minor, tiny) to change, or your own. See Points: styles and defaults
labels.styles.<name>.prioritynumberby styleWhich label wins when two overlap: higher wins. Built in: title 100, major 80, minor 40, tiny 10
labels.styles.<name>.overlapbooleanlabels.overlapLabels of this style are never hidden by another label
labels.overlapbooleanfalseNever hide a label when labels overlap. false hides the lower-priority one, which keeps busy scenes readable
labels.snapbooleanfalseRound label positions to whole px: crisper text on weak devices, steppier fast motion

A header bar ​

The card is the scene only: for an LCARS frame round it, put it in a Layout Card. An elbow or a bar-label button above the scene makes an "ALPHA QUADRANT" header.

  • The Layout Card places the astrometrics card and its frame together, so they move and size as one.

Performance and motion ​

The scene draws only when something moves, so an idle card costs nothing. Every astrometrics card and WebGL background on a page shares one WebGL context.

  • The scene draws when the camera moves, a fly-to runs, an object spins or an effect moves, the sky timer ticks or the theme changes. A still scene draws nothing, and off screen or in a hidden tab nothing draws.
  • Under reduced motion (the system setting), spin, auto_rotate, deformations and every effect (drift, scans, moving dashes, fills) stop, and a fly-to jumps instead of flying (no warp streaks).
  • Alert modes recolour the scene in place: every object crossfades to the alert palette over a moment (at once under reduced motion), the camera and motion carry on, and labels follow the palette at once.
More about drawing
  • The scene clock: a scene clock faster than a minute a second draws every frame; at a minute a second or slower it draws once a second. Under reduced motion the clock keeps running (it is data, like the sky) but moves things once a second.
  • Models: labels behind a model are hidden with a small depth image, made only while something moves. See model.
  • Alert modes and models: a model is built again in its new colours.

When the 3D view is unavailable ​

The card shows 3D VIEW UNAVAILABLE, with the reason, when it cannot draw the scene. That happens when the browser has no WebGL, or when the graphics context was lost (a GPU reset, too many WebGL pages).

  • Reloading the page starts the view again.
