Effects ​

The background animation presets, rebuilt as parts of the 3D scene: they sit in space and move with the camera instead of being a flat layer behind it. Every effect is still unless you give it a speed, so a scene with nothing moving draws nothing; under reduced motion (the system setting) they all hold still.

Real cards with sample entities. Drag to turn a tile: the clouds shift against the grid.

Nebula ​

type: nebula puts clouds of gas in the scene, in the look of the nebula preset with volumetric: true. Use it for a nebula on a star map, a gas cloud round a station, or a storm to fly into.

  • Each cloud is a few soft puffs facing the camera at slightly different depths, so as you turn the camera the clouds shift against each other and against the grids.
  • A cloud fades as you fly into it, and a planet hides the clouds behind it.
  • Every key of the object is in the nebula table; the sections below show what the volume, shapes, dust and presets do.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 340
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 20
      tilt: 25
      distance: 300
backdrop:
  stars:
    count: 2000
objects:
  - type: grid
    shape: disc
    radius: 100
    pattern: polar
    line_spacing: 10
    spoke_count: 36
  - type: nebula
    size: [220, 50, 220]
    count: 8
    seed: 5
    drift: 3
    colors: [var(--lcars-violet), var(--lcars-blue), var(--lcars-red)]
More about nebulae
  • Never tappable: clouds never catch a tap, so they never get in the way of points behind them. A label for a cloud is a point at the same place.
  • The seed: count clouds are placed by seed inside the region; the same seed always places the same clouds, and picks each cloud's colour from colors.
  • Entities: drift (and with render: volume, density) can follow an entity; it eases to each new value.
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
radiusnumber100The clouds fill a ball this big round at, scene units
sizenumber / [w, h, d]-Or a box, scene units (one number for a cube). Wins over radius
countnumber4How many clouds the seed places, 1 to 10
seednumber1The same seed always places the same clouds
colorscolour / listvar(--lcars-violet)One colour, or a list the seed picks from per cloud
driftnumber / [x, y]0How fast the gas churns, scene units per second. [x, y] gives a direction. 0 is still
Nebula keysclouds, color, min_radius, max_radius, brightness, turbulence, noise_scale, blend and the rest

Gas you can fly into ​

render: volume draws the same clouds (the same seed, colours and brightness) as one body of gas filling the region, instead of puffs. Fly into it and there is gas all round you, which puffs facing the camera cannot give.

  • The gas fades out toward the edges of the region, so its box or ball never shows.
  • A planet, model or other solid object inside the gas hides the gas behind it, and the gas in front of it still shows.
  • turbulence erodes the clouds into wisps and holes (0 gives smooth blobs), and density makes the gas thicker or thinner.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 340
camera:
  view: outside
  views:
    outside:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 20
      tilt: 20
      distance: 300
    inside:
      target: [40, 0, 10]
      yaw: 200
      tilt: 6
      distance: 30
backdrop:
  stars:
    count: 2000
points:
  # tap the labels to fly in and out
  - id: fly_in
    xyz: [40, 0, 10]
    label: FLY INSIDE
    tap_action:
      action: fly-to
      view: inside
  - id: fly_out
    xyz: [-90, 0, 0]
    label: BACK OUT
    tap_action:
      action: fly-to
      view: outside
objects:
  - type: grid
    shape: disc
    radius: 100
    pattern: polar
    line_spacing: 10
    spoke_count: 36
  - type: sphere
    at: [30, 0, -20]
    radius: 14
    fill:
      color: var(--lcars-orange)
  - type: nebula
    render: volume
    size: [220, 60, 220]
    count: 7
    seed: 5
    drift: 2
    colors: [var(--lcars-violet), var(--lcars-blue)]
More about volume gas
  • Bigger clouds: with render: volume, the clouds' default sizes are 0.25 and 0.55 of the region (min_radius, max_radius), so the gas fills it.
  • Resolution: a volume is drawn at half the screen's resolution, which soft gas does not show.
  • Volume only: shape, dust, density and lightning work only with render: volume.
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
renderstringpuffspuffs: soft puffs facing the camera. volume: one body of gas you can fly into
densitynumber1render: volume: how thick the gas is (2 is twice as thick)
turbulencenumber0.5How ragged the edges are, 0 to 1

Shapes, dust and colour ​

With render: volume, shape gives the gas a form, after the real nebulae: a hollow shell, an hourglass, long filaments, a cavity. The clouds still give the clumps and the colours inside the form, so count, seed and colors keep working; turn the form with rotation.

  • stretch draws each seeded cloud out (0 round to 1 long and flat), each turned its own way. The same seed with or without stretch places the same clouds.
  • core.color colours the gas deep inside a cloud (in a shape: near its middle), running out to the cloud colours at the edges, as in the Hubble pictures.
  • Dust is dark gas that hides the gas behind it: see Dust lanes.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 360
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 0
      tilt: 10
      distance: 420
backdrop:
  stars:
    count: 2000
objects:
  # the Ring nebula
  - type: nebula
    render: volume
    shape: shell
    at: [-150, 60, 0]
    radius: 55
    count: 8
    colors: [var(--lcars-ice), var(--lcars-violet)]
  # the Butterfly
  - type: nebula
    render: volume
    shape: bipolar
    at: [-50, 60, 0]
    radius: 55
    count: 8
    colors: [var(--lcars-orange), var(--lcars-red)]
    core:
      color: var(--lcards-moonlight)
  # the Veil
  - type: nebula
    render: volume
    shape: filaments
    at: [50, 60, 0]
    radius: 55
    count: 8
    stretch: 0.5
    colors: [var(--lcars-red), var(--lcars-ice)]
  # a blister, as in Orion
  - type: nebula
    render: volume
    shape: cavity
    at: [150, 60, 0]
    radius: 55
    count: 8
    colors: [var(--lcars-red), var(--lcars-orange)]
    core:
      color: var(--lcars-ice)
  # stretched clouds with dust across them
  - type: nebula
    render: volume
    at: [0, -70, 0]
    size: [300, 60, 80]
    count: 8
    stretch: 0.7
    dust:
      count: 3
    colors: [var(--lcars-violet), var(--lcars-blue)]
    core:
      color: var(--lcars-ice)
More about the shapes
  • clouds (the default): the clouds themselves.
  • shell: a thin hollow sphere, a ring with a dark middle from any side (the Ring nebula).
  • bipolar: an hourglass along the object's y axis, two hollow lobes joined at a narrow waist, the gas streaked outward (the Butterfly, the Hourglass).
  • filaments: long thin strands running along the object's x axis (the Veil).
  • cavity: a thick cup round a hollow that opens on the object's +z side: a bright bowl seen from there, a crescent from the side (a blister, as in Orion).
  • layers: a floor and a ceiling of gas across a box with a gap between: see the Badlands.
  • wake: a bright ribbon waving along the object's x axis and a sheet of gas trailing it: see the Nexus.
  • Hand-placed clouds take size and rotation instead of stretch: see Dust lanes.
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
shapestringcloudsrender: volume: the form of the gas: clouds, shell, bipolar, filaments, cavity, layers or wake
stretchnumber0How drawn out each seeded cloud is, 0 (round) to 1 (long and flat)
core.colorcolour-A colour deep in the gas, running out to the cloud colours at the edges. Left out: the cloud colours throughout

Dust lanes ​

Dust is dark gas in a volume nebula that hides the gas behind it: lay a few dark clouds across the gas with dust.count, or place one by hand with dust: true. A lane placed by hand through a bright cloud reads most clearly.

  • With blend: add (the default) the dust darkens only this nebula's own gas, so the stars behind still show through; with blend: normal it hides what is behind the nebula too.
  • Dust clouds the seed lays have a seed of their own, so adding dust moves no gas cloud.
  • clouds places clouds by hand, instead of count and the seed: up to 10 gas clouds and 4 dust clouds.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 300
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 0
      tilt: 0
      distance: 400
backdrop:
  stars:
    count: 2000
objects:
  - type: nebula
    render: volume
    radius: 130
    colors: [var(--lcars-orange)]
    clouds:
      # a bright cloud
      - xyz: [0, 0, 0]
        radius: 100
        opacity: 0.9
      # a long dark lane across it
      - xyz: [0, 0, 40]
        size: [200, 20, 30]
        rotation: [0, 0, 20]
        dust: true
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
dust.countnumber0Dark dust clouds the seed lays across the gas clouds, 0 to 4
dust.amountnumber0.8How dark the dust is, 0 to 1
dust.colorcolourblackThe dust's colour
cloudslist-Clouds placed by hand, instead of count and the seed
clouds[].xyz[x, y, z][0, 0, 0]Where the cloud sits, scene units from the object
clouds[].radiusnumberhalfway between min_radius and max_radiusCloud radius, scene units
clouds[].size[w, h, d]-Or an ellipsoid, scene units. Replaces radius
clouds[].rotation[x, y, z]-Turns the ellipsoid, degrees, as an object's rotation
clouds[].colorcolourthe next of colorsThis cloud's colour. Without it, the clouds take colors in order
clouds[].opacitynumberthe middle of brightnessOpacity, 0 to 1
clouds[].dustbooleanfalserender: volume: a dark dust cloud (dust.amount, dust.color) instead of glowing gas

The Badlands: layers, columns and lightning ​

The Badlands, the plasma storms of Deep Space Nine and Voyager, start from a preset: preset: badlands (Deep Space Nine) or preset: badlands_voyager (Voyager). Fly the camera into the gap between the sheets.

  • badlands: liquid fire in yellow, orange and red, an orange band glowing between the sheets, fire columns thick and thin.
  • badlands_voyager: peach and mauve layers, held pink lightning columns, strikes that light the gas.
  • Every key you set wins over the preset's; in band, columns and lightning key by key, so columns: { count: 3 } keeps the preset's kind and colour.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 360
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 30
      tilt: 3
      distance: 110
backdrop:
  stars:
    count: 2000
objects:
  - type: nebula
    preset: badlands
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 360
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 200
      tilt: 3
      distance: 160
backdrop:
  stars:
    count: 2000
objects:
  - type: nebula
    preset: badlands_voyager
More about the Badlands
  • What the presets are: both are render: volume with shape: layers in a size: [500, 140, 500] box (Voyager's 160 high): a floor and a ceiling of gas with their surfaces rolled into ridges, colors running from each sheet's surface into its depth.
  • Deep Space Nine's sets veins: 0.8, a narrower gap of 0.4, a brighter band and eight fire columns from 1 to 3 times the usual width.
  • Voyager's sets seven lightning columns, a butter-coloured band and strikes in the gas.
  • Your own box: the preset's box is 500 across; with a size (or radius) of your own, its drift scales with it, so the gas churns as fast for its size. A drift you set stays as you wrote it.
  • Turning parts off: band: false or lightning: false drops that part of the preset; true keeps the preset's settings.
  • Lightning anywhere: lightning works on any volume nebula, not only layers, and a lightning object striking inside any volume nebula lights its gas too. The strikes take the nebula's seed unless you give them one.
  • Reduced motion: the columns stand still and no strike shows.
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
presetstring-badlands, badlands_voyager (or nexus, below): a named nebula to start from
gapnumber0.56shape: layers: how much of the height is open between the sheets, 0 (they touch) to 0.9
veinsnumber0shape: layers: bright fine veins in the sheets' surfaces with darker gas between (liquid fire), 0 to 1
bandboolean / objectfalseshape: layers: light glowing in the gap, brightest in a thin sheet at its middle
band.brightnessnumber1How bright, 0 to 3
band.widthnumber0.08How far the glow reaches from the middle, a fraction of half the height
band.colorcolourvar(--lcards-moonlight)The band's colour
columnsobject-shape: layers: columns from the floor to the ceiling: see Columns
lightningboolean / objectfalseStrikes inside the gas that light it: true, or the lightning object's rate, flash, color, glow, width, jaggedness, branches, brightness and seed

Columns ​

columns stands fire, vortices or held lightning from the floor to the ceiling of a layers nebula, spread over the region. A field of vortices, with the funnels joined into columns, is a storm of its own.

  • fire is rising flame, wide at the foot with a splash ring on the floor; its white-hot heart takes columns.color and its edge the sheets' surface colour.
  • vortex is a twisting funnel, narrow at its middle (waist) and dark at its rim.
  • lightning is a held, crawling bolt that lights the gas round it.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 360
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 200
      tilt: 4
      distance: 150
backdrop:
  stars:
    count: 2000
objects:
  - type: nebula
    render: volume
    shape: layers
    size: [500, 160, 500]
    count: 6
    seed: 8
    drift: [3, 0]
    colors: [var(--lcars-violet-creme), var(--lcars-violet), var(--lcars-blue)]
    band:
      brightness: 0.8
      color: var(--lcars-violet-creme)
    columns:
      count: 6
      kind: vortex
      waist: 0.4
      width: 1.5
      color: var(--lcars-violet-creme)
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
columns.kindstringfirefire, vortex or lightning
columns.countnumber4How many, 1 to 10, spread over the region
columns.widthnumber / [min, max]1fire and vortex: how thick, times the usual. A pair gives each column its own width between them, so thick columns and thin strands stand together
columns.waistnumber0.2vortex: how wide the middle is against the ends, 0.05 (two funnels meeting at a point) to 1 (one straight column)
columns.speednumber1lightning: how fast the bolts crawl and flicker. 0.5 is half as fast, 0 still
columns.colorcolourvar(--lcards-moonlight)The fire's white-hot heart, the vortex, or the bolt
columns.glow.colorcolourthe column colourlightning: the glow round the bolts

The Nexus: a ribbon and its wake ​

The Nexus, the ribbon of energy in Star Trek Generations, starts from preset: nexus: a white-hot ribbon waving across a long box, a wake of pink and red gas streaming out behind it, and small discharges crawling off it and lighting the gas.

  • The preset is render: volume with shape: wake in a size: [2000, 500, 500] box: the ribbon waves along the object's x axis near the bottom of the region and the gas trails up from it, colors running from the ribbon out, white-hot right at it.
  • Every key you set wins over the preset's; in ribbon, wake and lightning key by key.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 360
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, -60, 0]
      yaw: 8
      tilt: 6
      distance: 1500
backdrop:
  stars:
    count: 3000
objects:
  - type: nebula
    preset: nexus
More about the Nexus
  • Your own box: with a size (or radius) of your own, the ribbon's speed scales with its length, so the waves run as fast for its size. A speed you set stays as you wrote it.
  • Discharges: lightning.mode: crawl (the preset's) puts short bolts and small loops on the ribbon that flicker and jump along it, in place of strikes. It works only with shape: wake.
  • The glow's colour defaults to the first of colors.
  • Reduced motion: the ribbon holds still, and the discharges stand still and dim.
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
ribbonboolean / objecttrueshape: wake: the bright line and the curve it follows. false: the gas only
ribbon.wavesnumber2.5How many waves along it, end to end
ribbon.amplitudenumber0.2How far it waves, a fraction of half the region's height (a little less in depth), up to 0.5
ribbon.speednumber0 (the preset: 40)How fast the waves run along it, scene units a second; the wake streams out from it as fast. Negative runs the other way
ribbon.widthnumber6 (the preset: 4)The line's width, px
ribbon.colorcolourvar(--lcards-moonlight)The line's colour, and the gas right at it
ribbon.glowboolean / objecttrueThe glow round the line and in the gas near it
ribbon.glow.colorcolourthe first of colorsThe glow's colour
ribbon.glow.widthnumber8How wide the outer glow is, times the line's width
wake.sidestringupWhich way the gas trails: up, or down (the ribbon near the top). Turn the object with rotation to trail it another way
wake.leannumber0How far the gas leans along the ribbon as it goes out, -1 to 1: a fan trailing to one side, seen from afar
lightning.modestringstrike (the preset: crawl)crawl: discharges crawling off the ribbon. strike: bolts in the gas
lightning.countnumber6crawl: how many at once, 1 to 40
lightning.reachnumber0.35crawl: how far they stand off the ribbon, a fraction of half the height
lightning.speednumber1crawl: how fast they flicker, reshape and jump

A travelling Nexus ​

To send the Nexus travelling, put it at: a moving point with face: motion. Facing turns the object's +z along the travel, so rotation: [-90, 0, 0] turns the wake (its +y) to trail behind.

yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 360
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 0
      tilt: 35
      distance: 7000
backdrop:
  stars:
    count: 3000
points:
  - id: nexus
    polar: [2500, 0]
    object: track
    label: NEXUS
objects:
  # the point rides this spinning ring
  - type: ring
    id: track
    radius: 2500
    spin: 3
    width: 0.5
    opacity: 0.15
  - type: nebula
    preset: nexus
    at: nexus
    size: [1500, 400, 400]
    face: motion
    rotation: [-90, 0, 0]
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
Keys every object takesat, face and rotation; see Facing the way it goes

Scan bands ​

scan on a grid, sphere, volume or model sends a bright band across its lines, as the background grid's scan_period does. Use it for a sensor sweep across a map, a planet being scanned, or a ship's hologram.

  • A sphere sweeps from pole to pole, a dome from the top to its rim, a cylinder top to bottom, and a plane runs south to north.
  • A polar disc rings out from its centre like a sensor ping, and a volume sweeps down its walls.
  • On a model the band runs along its longest side, in the hologram look.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 320
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 10, 0]
      yaw: 20
      tilt: 25
      distance: 260
objects:
  # a ping ringing out from the centre
  - type: grid
    shape: disc
    radius: 100
    pattern: polar
    line_spacing: 10
    spoke_count: 36
    scan:
      period: 6
      color: var(--lcars-yellow)
  # pole to pole
  - type: sphere
    at: [-50, 25, 0]
    radius: 22
    color: var(--lcars-blue)
    scan:
      period: 5
  # down the walls
  - type: volume
    shape: sector
    radius: [55, 95]
    angle: [100, 170]
    height: [0, 30]
    color: var(--lcars-green)
    scan:
      period: 4
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
scan.periodnumber0Seconds per sweep. 0: none. Stops under reduced motion
scan.colorcolourvar(--lcards-moonlight)The band's colour

Moving dashes and pulses ​

A dashed line can move its dashes along it, and any line can send bright pulses along it, as the flow preset's streaks do. Use it for traffic on a route, power along a conduit, or a ring that turns.

  • dash with pattern and speed moves the dashes on a ring, path or trail, speed px per second (negative runs backwards). Dashes keep their px size at any distance, so they move at the same speed on screen near or far.
  • pulse works on a ring, path, trail and beam. Pulses add light, so on a coloured line they burn toward white.
  • With dash.speed set, the pulses move at that speed too; else at pulse.speed.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 340
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 20
      tilt: 40
      distance: 320
points:
  - id: hub
    xyz: [0, 0, 0]
    marker: diamond
    label: HUB
    style: major
  - id: alpha
    polar: [70, 30]
    marker: dot
    label: ALPHA
  - id: beta
    polar: [60, 150]
    marker: dot
    label: BETA
  - id: gamma
    polar: [80, 260]
    marker: dot
    label: GAMMA
objects:
  - type: grid
    shape: disc
    radius: 100
    pattern: polar
    line_spacing: 20
    spoke_count: 12
    opacity: 0.5
  # pulses with the defaults
  - type: path
    from: hub
    to: alpha
    arc: 10
    color: var(--lcars-orange)
    pulse: true
  # moving dashes
  - type: path
    from: hub
    to: beta
    arc: 10
    color: var(--lcars-orange)
    dash:
      pattern: [8, 6]
      speed: 30
  # longer, rarer yellow pulses
  - type: path
    from: hub
    to: gamma
    arc: 10
    width: 2
    color: var(--lcars-blue)
    pulse:
      length: 24
      gap: 120
      color: var(--lcars-yellow)
  # a ring whose dashes turn
  - type: ring
    radius: 100
    width: 2
    dash:
      pattern: [12, 8]
      speed: 20
KeyTypeDefaultDescription
dash.patternlistsolidDashes as [on, off] in px (more pairs repeat)
dash.speednumber0Move the dashes (and pulses) along the line, px per second; negative runs backwards
pulseboolean / objectoffBright pulses moving along the line: true, or their settings
Line keyspulse.length (14), pulse.gap (90), pulse.width, pulse.color (var(--lcards-moonlight)) and pulse.speed (80)

Animated surface fills ​

fill.effect paints an animated pattern on a sphere's body or under a grid's lines: a star's surface, a gas giant's bands, an ocean, a density map. The types are the plasma, fluid and contour-field presets, plus bands (belts round the latitude, a gas giant) and swirl (gas spiralling in, below).

  • The pattern is drawn in 3D, so it has no seam round a sphere and no pinch at its poles, and it turns with spin.
  • On a sphere it is painted over fill.color in place of image, and the body still hides what is behind it.
  • preset starts from a planet type; the keys you set win over it.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 320
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 10, 0]
      yaw: 15
      tilt: 30
      distance: 230
objects:
  # contour bands under a grid's lines
  - type: grid
    shape: plane
    size: [220, 140]

More about fills
  • Detail: base_frequency is features per scene unit, about 3 across the object's radius by default; num_octaves adds layers of finer detail.
  • Motion: with speed above 0 the pattern flows through the object; under reduced motion it holds still.
  • Entities: the fill's numbers and colours can follow entities, as on any object: see Live data.

Swirls ​

type: swirl is gas spiralling in round the object's y axis: a whirlpool, an accretion disc, a wormhole's mouth. On a well grid the gas pours down the funnel; on a disc it lies flat.

  • It turns faster toward the middle and is streaked along the spiral, with dark lanes of thinner gas between the streaks, a rippled heart and a ragged outer edge that trails off in wisps (turbulence).
  • colors run from the heart out to the rim, and speed is how fast it turns at the rim.
  • Set line_width: 0 and edges: false on the grid to show the gas alone.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 340
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, -2, 0]
      yaw: 20
      tilt: 40
      distance: 34
objects:
  - type: grid
    shape: well
    well:
      throat: 0.07
    radius: 15
    depth: 5
    line_width: 0
    edges: false
    fill:
      effect:
        type: swirl
        colors: [var(--lcards-moonlight), var(--lcards-orange-lightest), var(--lcars-violet), var(--lcards-blue-medium), var(--lcars-violet), var(--lcards-blue-dark)]
        speed: 3
        turbulence: 0.8
        num_octaves: 5
  - type: glow
    at: [0, 3, 0]
    size: 10
    color: var(--lcards-orange-lightest)
More about swirls
  • A whole wormhole: the Bajoran wormhole starter builds a mouth from a swirl, with a shaft of light, sparkling stars and wisps of gas.

Warp and dust ​

A fly-to with warp streaks the stars past the camera, and backdrop.dust fills the space round the camera with faint particles that show depth on every move. The streaks grow with the camera's speed and shrink back to stars as it lands.

  • Turn warp on for every fly-to with camera.warp: true, for one view with warp: true in that view (a point's or object's view takes it too), or for one action with warp: true on its fly-to (or the lcards.fly_to action); false there turns it off.
  • The streaks follow how fast the camera moves for its distance from what it looks at, so a hop between planets streaks as a jump across the galaxy does.
  • The dust stays put as the camera moves, unlike the stars at infinity, and streaks in a warp too. It has no edge: particles fade out at radius from the camera and new ones fade in ahead as it travels, and coming back to a place shows the same dust.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 340
camera:
  view: far
  fly_duration: 2400
  views:
    far:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 20
      tilt: 20
      distance: 600
    near:
      target: [40, 5, 30]
      yaw: 30
      tilt: 10
      distance: 25
      warp: true
  buttons: true
backdrop:
  stars:
    count: 4000
    max_radius: 1.4
    spectrum: true
  dust:
    count: 1500
points:
  # tap the label to warp in
  - id: core
    xyz: [0, 0, 0]
    label: GALACTIC CORE
    style: title
    tap_action:
      action: fly-to
      view: near
objects:
  - type: grid
    shape: disc
    radius: 100
    pattern: polar
    line_spacing: 10
    spoke_count: 36
    opacity: 0.6
  # tap the glow to fly back out
  - type: glow
    at: core
    size: 50
    color: var(--lcars-yellow)
    tap_action:
      action: fly-to
      view: far
More about warp and dust
  • Following a toggle: camera.warp with an entity makes fly-tos streak while it is on (on, true, yes, engaged, open, home, or a number above 0), such as an input_boolean.
  • Spectrum: backdrop.stars.spectrum: true splits each streak into colour, as a Doppler shift of the star's own colour: violet toward the tail (nearer the direction of flight) and red toward the head. White stars show the whole rainbow; a blue star streaks green to violet, its tail fading as it shifts past violet, and a red star the other way. spectrum.direction: reverse puts violet at the head instead.
  • Denser dust: a smaller radius is denser and shows depth on smaller moves.
  • Reduced motion: nothing streaks.

Going to warp ​

warp on the card sends the ship to warp where it is: the scene's own backdrop stars and dust streak along the view, and the warp starfield streams past behind the scene. false drops out of warp again; both warp and warp_factor can follow entities, so a toggle and a slider make an ENGAGE button and a warp factor control.

  • warp reads on, true, yes, engaged, open, home or a number above 0 as engaged.
  • Going to warp takes about two seconds, dropping out a little less; a change of warp factor eases too.
  • camera.warp is different: it streaks the stars only while a fly-to moves the camera.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-astrometrics-card
height: 320
camera:
  views:
    v:
      target: [0, 0, 0]
      yaw: 0
      tilt: 10
      distance: 120
backdrop:
  stars:
    count: 3000
    spectrum: true
  dust:
    count: 1500
# at warp while the toggle is on: tap ENGAGE
warp:
  entity: input_boolean.demo_toggle
# the warp factor from a slider, 1 to 9.9
warp_factor:
  entity: input_number.lcards_test_power
  output: [1, 9.9]
points:
  - id: engage
    xyz: [0, 0, 0]
    marker: square
    style: major
    label: ENGAGE (TAP)
    tap_action:
      action: toggle
      entity: input_boolean.demo_toggle
More about going to warp
  • With spectrum: with backdrop.stars.spectrum, the backdrop streaks are in colour.
  • Reduced motion: nothing streams.

Cost ​

A still effect costs nothing between frames, because a still scene draws nothing; an effect that moves costs a little on every frame. Most scenes stay well inside the time a smooth frame allows.

  • A volume nebula costs more than puffs, and a little more again seen from inside the gas. A shape, stretch, core.color or dust adds a little on top.
  • The Badlands are the costliest effect, most of all from inside with columns and veins; their lightning adds little.
  • The Nexus costs little more than a plain volume nebula.
  • The nebula and fill shaders load in a small extra download the first time a card uses one.
