Designing LCARS Screens ​

What makes a screen read as LCARS, written down as guidelines you can pick from. They come from studying the screens people recognise: the Star Trek: Picard season 3 consoles (as rebuilt by meWho's Titan.DS and LCARSlad London) and production panels from The Next Generation, Deep Space Nine and Voyager.

None of this is required. A dashboard that follows three of these guidelines already looks more like LCARS than one that follows none; pick the ones that suit your screen. Building a Dashboard covers the mechanics (which card for which job, the layout view); this page is about the look.

Two looks ​

Picard (Titan.DS, Picard S3)Classic (TNG, DS9, Voyager)
StructureThin frames in a few dark, muted tonesThick blocks of colour
ColourMostly black and structure tones; a little accentSeveral pastel colours at once
ButtonsSmall square blocks with code numbers, pills at the edgesGrids of rounded pills, led by big numbers
Main displayLine-drawn schematic inside a nested frameContent inside a bracket or sweep frame
Themes that suit itLCARS Picard [LCARdS Red/Blue Accent], LCARS Picard I/II, LCARS 25CLCARS Default, LCARS TNG, LCARS Classic, LCARS Nemesis Blue

Most of the guidelines below apply to both. The look mostly comes from the theme: the same layout reads as Picard in a Picard theme and as TNG in a classic one.

Colour comes from your theme ​

Don't pick colours for an LCARS screen; pick roles, and let the theme supply the colour. Then the screen follows when you switch themes, into light or dark mode, and into alert mode.

RoleUse forTokenHA-LCARS variable
Main structureSweeps, frame bars, the most important frametheme:colors.ui.primary--lcars-ui-primary
Secondary structureBlock columns, side barstheme:colors.ui.secondary, theme:colors.ui.quaternary--lcars-ui-secondary, --lcars-ui-quaternary
Dark structureInner frames, inactive blocks, tabstheme:colors.card.topColor, theme:colors.card.bottomColor--lcars-card-top-color, --lcars-card-bottom-color
AccentWhat's on, selected or needs attentiontheme:colors.ui.tertiary, theme:colors.card.buttonOn--lcars-ui-tertiary, --lcars-card-button-color
DataReadings, schematic lines, gaugesa palette colour, e.g. var(--lcars-blue, var(--lcards-blue-medium))
AlertAlert statesvar(--lcars-alert-color, var(--lcards-orange-dark))
Text on a roleLabels on a coloured blockthe role's text partner: --lcars-ui-primary-text, --lcars-card-top-text, ...

Prefer the theme: tokens. They resolve to your HA-LCARS theme's variable when it's installed and fall back to the LCARdS palette when it isn't, so the card always has a colour. A bare var(--lcars-...) is empty without HA-LCARS: give it a fallback, as in the table. HA-LCARS themes have one accent role, so the data colour is a deliberate pick from the palette: check it reads on your theme's structure colours.

  • Keep accent colour sparse. In the Picard references only about a tenth of the screen is accent colour; the rest is black and structure tones. A block stays a structure colour unless it means something.
  • Classic screens are more colourful: several roles side by side, mixed freely. The classic themes already set their roles to the pastel set.
  • Light and dark mode belong to the theme. If your theme has both, a screen built from roles works in both.
  • A view can choose its own theme: theme: LCARS Default on a view shows that view in that theme, whatever the user's profile theme is. Handy for a classic screen on an otherwise Picard dashboard.

Structure ​

  • Black canvas. Content sits on black inside frames, not on coloured blocks.
  • Sweeps make the frames. A frame is a bar that turns 90° into another bar (an elbow). Two sweeps facing each other (a header pair, or a left and a right sweep) is the signature LCARS shape; a lone elbow in a corner is rarer.
  • Nest frames. Put a thinner frame inside the main one around the most important content. Picard screens nest two thin frames; classic ones put a thin sweep inside a thick one.
  • Break long bars into segments of different lengths, separated by small black gaps. A segment can carry a small label or number.
  • One gap size everywhere. The same gap between blocks, segments and frames on a screen (grid-gap on the layout view and layout cards). Blocks never touch.
  • Labels sit right-aligned, usually at the bottom right of their block, in condensed capitals (the theme font). Numbers right-aligned in columns.
  • One title, top right, large, in the accent colour or white; a dim subtitle at the bottom right if you need one. Classic titles often read NAME • NUMBER.
  • Numbers as texture. Code-like numbers (13-650, 0099•06) fill blocks and columns, and dense number cascades fill spare areas. Real readings and decorative numbers look alike; that's part of the look.
  • A sweep is at least as wide as what it frames. If a sweep's vertical bar is narrower than the column above it, it looks detached; close a narrow column with a segmented bar instead.
  • Fill space with structure, not black. If an area is empty, put blocks, a number cascade or a segmented bar in it.

Picard ​

  • Columns of small number blocks down one or both sides: mostly structure tones, a few in the accent, some dark (inactive). See code-block column.
  • A utility column of small dark pills, housed inside a bracket frame at the far edge (zoom, settings, dimmer), with a red alert pill at the bottom. See utility pills.
  • Concentric sweeps: a thick sweep with a thin line running parallel inside it, for headers and corners. See concentric sweep.
  • Controls inside a wide arm: a thick frame whose black inset holds a group of blocks. See controls inside a wide arm.
  • Vertical gauges with tick scales beside the number columns (Picard and gauge sliders).
  • The main display is a schematic: a line drawing with leader lines to labels, small accent highlights for faults (MSD).
  • A strip of tabs and sliders along the bottom. Tabs show their state as a line along the top (toggle-tab).
  • Fixed chrome, changing centre. Titan.DS keeps the same frame, columns and strips on every screen and swaps only the central display. In LCARdS: a layout view whose centre area holds several cards with visibility conditions, one shown at a time.

Classic (TNG, DS9, Voyager) ​

  • Colour blocks are the structure. Thick header blocks, often in several roles side by side.
  • Grids of rounded pills, each with a small code label, in column groups that alternate two or three roles. See pill grid.
  • A column of big numbers (20, 008, 358) leads each group of pills.
  • Bracket frames: a top and a bottom bar with rounded ends, and a sidebar of stacked blocks with code numbers. See bracket frame.
  • A split header: two sweeps meeting across the top, with a thin segmented band in the gap between them.
  • Segment the long arms. A classic bar seldom runs unbroken across the screen: the sweep turns into a short bar, then segments of different lengths and roles carry labels. See segmented bar.

Motion and alerts ​

  • At rest, move small things only: ticking numbers, a dot row, a scanning line. On the reference screens about one twentieth of the screen moves at a time. Animations, data grid cascades.
  • Animate entrances once (on_show with draw, cascade or text-reveal), not continuously.
  • Alerts change the whole screen at once: alert mode recolours everything in place, and an alert overlay adds the alert symbol. Avoid making every card flash.
  • Show state by colour in place, not by moving things: a block turns to the accent, a tab's line lights up.

Screen archetypes ​

Start a screen by picking its overall shape, then fill the areas with patterns. These are the layouts that recur across the reference screens; each one suits a different job. Mix them: a Picard screen can use the wings layout, a classic one the tile wall. Take groupings and sections from the gallery and the references freely, but compose the screen for its own job rather than reproducing one whole.

ArchetypeGood forShape (grid-template-areas)In the gallery
Display with side columnsOne main system (a ship, a floor plan, a chart) with controls around it; Titan.DS modules"pills top top top" "pills left main right" "pills left strip right"Picard
Display over controlsA wide display with a dense control deck under it; Titan.DS ship screens"head head" "display display" "deck1 deck2"Picard
WingsTwo related halves (port and starboard, upstairs and downstairs) around a shared centre"head head head" "lwing centre rwing"
Portrait panelPhones and wall tablets mounted upright"title" "main" "gauges" "controls"Picard
Tile wallMany similar things at once: cameras, rooms, sensors"head head head" "t1 t2 t3" "t4 t5 t6"
Sweep consoleA classic control panel: thick header sweep, sidebar, button groups"head head head" "side pills readout" "foot foot foot"Classic
Bracket screenA readout or a document: capped top and bottom bars, a numbered sidebar"top top" "side main" "bottom bottom"Classic
ListRosters, schedules, logs: rows of text under a split header"head head" "list numbers" "pills pills"Classic

Two things vary a screen more than anything else: which archetype you start from, and which patterns you put in its areas. A screen that reuses one gallery screen's grid with new labels is the same screen; one that combines a different archetype with two or three patterns is a new one.

Patterns ​

Building blocks to compose a screen from. Each is a layout card you can drop into a layout view area, with every colour from a theme role. Change the numbers, labels and entities to yours.

Two things keep a pattern from collapsing when you change it: give the layout card's rows a size (grid-template-rows or grid-auto-rows in px or minmax(0, 1fr); rows left to size themselves squash the cards), and keep a button's row at least as tall as the button (56 px by default; small pills and blocks set a lower min_height, bar labels size their text to it).

Concentric sweep ​

A thick sweep with a thin line running parallel inside it, the Picard header and corner shape. A segmented elbow: outer_segment is the sweep, inner_segment the line. Swap the weights for the other common Picard form: a thin outer band with a large curve (outer_segment about 40 by 14, outer_curve around 120) wrapped round a wide inner block (inner_segment 150 by 30), as in the lcars-shell strategy and the split screen.

yaml
type: custom:lcards-elbow
grid_options: { rows: 3 }
elbow:
  type: header-left
  style: segmented
  segments:
    gap: 6
    outer_segment: { bar_width: 140, bar_height: 24, color: theme:colors.ui.secondary }
    inner_segment: { bar_width: 6, bar_height: 4, color: theme:colors.ui.primary }
text:
  title:
    content: COURSE SELECT
    position: bottom-right
    color: theme:colors.ui.tertiary

Segmented bar (classic arms) ​

Classic screens rarely run one unbroken bar across the screen: the sweep turns into a short bar, then the bar continues as segments of different lengths and roles, each with a label. Keep the elbow's area just wider than its vertical bar, and put the segments in a row the same height as the bar.

A bar that carries the screen or section title, with a short cap before it. Picard sections (MISSION OPS, PROPULSION) use this instead of a sweep. The title grows to fill the bar's height, so in a narrow area set a smaller text.label.font_size (or a shorter min_height) or it runs off the end.

min_height: 48

    content: MISSION OPS
    color: theme:colors.ui.tertiary
style:
  card:
    color:
      background:
        default: theme:colors.ui.tertiary

Putting it together ​

A screen is a layout view shaped by one of the archetypes, with patterns in its areas: for example a facing-sweeps header across the top, a code-block column and utility pills down the left, a nested frame with an MSD or chart in the centre, a toggle tab strip along the bottom. Or a portrait panel: a header bar with a title, a gauge bank, a selector strip. The Dashboard Gallery has six complete screens built this way, three per look, each from a different archetype.
