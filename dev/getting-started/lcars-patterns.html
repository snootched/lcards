LCARS Patterns ​

Building blocks to compose an LCARS screen from: each one is a card or a layout card you can drop into an area of a layout view, with every colour taken from a theme role. Change the numbers, labels and entities to yours. Designing LCARS Screens explains what makes a screen read as LCARS, and its archetypes give the overall shapes to put these in.

  • Patterns are modules with slots. A slot holds a decorative part or a control of the same size: a code block can toggle a light, a gauge can follow a real sensor, a number in a cascade can be a reading. Swap them freely; the module keeps its shape.
  • Small parts keep a fixed size. The examples use the sizes measured on the reference screens (blocks about 64 x 24 px, letters about half the block height, font_size 13 to 14) on px tracks, so they keep that size on a full screen; only a main display takes minmax(0, 1fr). See Scale and density.
  • Give the layout card's rows a size (grid-template-rows or grid-auto-rows in px or minmax(0, 1fr)): rows left to size themselves squash the cards.
  • Keep a button's row at least as tall as the button: 56 px by default. Small pills and blocks set a lower min_height, and bar labels size their text to it.
  • Patterns marked (classic) come from TNG, DS9 and Voyager screens, and most of the others from the Picard references. The theme sets the colours, so most work in either look.
  • The examples pick every colour by hand to show the look. Before you copy them onto controls bound to your entities, read Colours by state or by hand.

Code-block column ​

The Picard side column: small blocks with code numbers, mostly in structure tones, one in the accent. Use it down one or both sides of a screen.

  • The blocks are panels (panel-light, panel-dark) with the label moved to the middle right and min_height: 0, on 64 x 24 px tracks. Two columns side by side is the usual Picard width.
  • A panel with an entity shows the theme's accent while it's on and the dark structure tone while it's off: toggle the light under the card to see block 42-991 change.
  • Leave out text.label.content and each block gets a random code.
  • Picard columns often break the blocks with a short group of three or four rows of small numbers: a number cascade in the column's width.

Toggle tab strip ​

A row of tabs whose state shows as a line along the top: dark when off, the accent when on. The toggle-tab preset shows the entity's name in the middle; a tab without an entity takes its text.name.content.

Facing sweeps header ​

Two sweeps meeting across the top, with the title in the black space between them. The signature LCARS header: a header-left and a header-right elbow side by side, each in its own role.

Display sweep ​

The frame round a Picard main display: one sweep that runs along the top, turns down the left and back along the bottom, open on the right. The top and bottom bars run on past the display as segments or dot rows; the display (an MSD, a chart) fills the black inside.

On a full screen the usual form is wider: the header bar turns down over a code-block column, the column is the display's left side, and a short sweep at its foot turns into the closing bar under the display. The frame below is the compact form for a display on its own.

  • The bars end square on the open side, and thin bars keep one width round the corners: leave inner_curve unset.
  • An elbow draws its inner curve only when its area is wider than its bar_width (and taller than its bar_height). When a header or foot sweep sits over a code column, give its area a gutter of about 40 px past the column, or it fills with a slab.
  • Keep the bars thin (10 to 16 px) with a large outer curve; a code-block column often sits just left of the sweep.
  • To run the bars on past the display, place the frame across the first columns of the area and the display over the rest, then put segments or a dot row in line with the top and bottom bars.
  • One sweep per corner: the display sweep's top bar can be the header's bar. Don't stack a separate header corner right above the sweep's own corner, or the two curves read as a double ring.
  • A closed frame on all four sides, or two nested rings (style: segmented with segments.inner_frame), is not seen on the reference screens; use it only when a screen asks for a boxed display.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-elbow
grid_options:
  rows: 4
elbow:
  type: frame
  segment:
    color: theme:colors.ui.secondary
  frame:
    bar_width: 14
    bar_height: 12
    outer_curve: 60
    right:
      enabled: false

Labelled schematic ​

The main display: a dimmed line drawing with small labels pointing at its parts. It is an MSD whose labels are text-only buttons placed as controls, each joined to its part by a leader line.

  • Widen view_box beyond the drawing to leave room for the labels, and place each label in that margin, well inside the view_box edge, with its proportions matching the display's area (otherwise the drawing is letterboxed and the margin moves).
  • A leader leaves its label from the side that faces the part (anchor_side: left or right), at the text's mid-height. Align the text toward that side (center-right or center-left) and size the box close to the text, so the line starts at the text, not at empty box.
  • Place the label so the part lies well past its end, above or below it: the line runs out, turns once near the part, and goes in. Leave top and bottom exits to a bar's corner.
  • Sizes are in view_box units and scale with the drawing. Aim for labels that render at 12 to 14 px, lines at 2 to 3 px (Picard) or 1.5 to 2 px (classic), and bends that read as curves: value = px x view_box width / the display's width in px. Use one leader style for the whole MSD, with one corner size and line width; mix styles only when asked.

More about leader lines
  • Picard bends are tight but never square: a small corner_radius (about 8 to 12 px rendered), never corner_style: miter. The bracket style is the exception: its lines are straight, or turn once at a sharp corner into a diagonal.
  • Picard styles, one per MSD: straight out of the side with one tight bend (above); a thin bar beside every label, its line leaving the bar's top corner straight or into one diagonal (below); or a short stub and then a straight run at an angle (route: angled, stub_length; see routing modes). A fault keeps the screen's style and takes the accent.
  • Gap: the button's text padding (8 by default) plus anchor_gap keeps the line off the letters. A line leaving a bar or a filled block touches it.
  • End on a point: attach_to as [x, y] on the drawing. A line that ends on an overlay (a shape or control) arrives at the side nearest its label, which adds a bend; give it attach_side: center to keep the one bend.
  • Faults take the accent, the line too; everything else stays in structure tones.
  • A grid behind the drawing (the grid background preset) suits it, but that preset scrolls by default: set its config.scroll_speed_x and config.scroll_speed_y to 0 so the screen stays still.

The bracket style gives every label a thin bar on the side its line leaves, and the line runs from the bar's top corner along its edge, then straight to the part. The bar is the label's own border (style.border.width.right or left); anchor_side: top-right or top-left with route_hint: xy starts the line along the top, flush with the bar. Lay the labels out one of two ways: in columns beside the drawing, each level with its part so every line runs straight out; or above and below it, each line running a long stub (about 60 px) along the bar's edge before one sharp turn into a straight diagonal (route: angled, stub_length, corner_style: miter). No curves. The line takes the bar's colour.

Utility pills ​

The Picard edge column: a bracket frame open on the outer side, a few small dark pills, and red alert at the bottom. The frame and a layout card of pills share one grid area, and the inner layout card's padding keeps the pills inside the frame.

  • The bracket's bars end square on the open side; only the corners where a bar turns are rounded. A frame does this by itself when a side is off.
  • Pills are for the few utility controls only (zoom, settings, dimmer, red alert: three to five). Other buttons on a Picard screen are rectangular blocks.
  • The empty text-only button takes up the flexible row, so the red alert pill sits at the bottom whatever the height. On a full screen that row is a slot: a gauge pair or a code-block column fits there.
  • RED ALERT runs lcards.red_alert: tap it here and the page goes to red alert.

Concentric sweep ​

A thick sweep with a thin line running parallel inside it: the Picard header and corner shape. It is a segmented elbow (style: segmented): outer_segment is the sweep and inner_segment the line.

  • Leave the inner segment's curves unset: the line then stays concentric with the sweep and keeps one even width round the corner.

  • Use it where nothing else turns the same corner: above a display sweep, let the display's own sweep make the corner instead.

  • The line ends where the sweep's bar ends. When the bar carries on as segments in the next cards, end the elbow's area at the first gap, so the line stops at that gap with the bar.

  • Keep the sweep's vertical part short: let the bar carry on as a segmented band beside a column of small blocks rather than as one tall block, or a wide inner block becomes a full-height slab.

  • Swap the weights for the other common Picard form: a thin outer band with a large curve wrapped round a wide inner block. The split screen uses outer_segment 40 by 14 with outer_curve: 100 round an inner_segment 134 by 24 (as wide as a two-block column); the lcars-shell strategy's corners have the same form.

yaml
type: custom:lcards-elbow
grid_options:
  rows: 3
elbow:
  type: header-left
  style: segmented
  segments:
    gap: 6
    outer_segment:
      bar_width: 140
      bar_height: 24
      color: theme:colors.ui.secondary
    inner_segment:
      bar_width: 6
      bar_height: 4
      color: theme:colors.ui.primary
text:
  title:
    content: COURSE SELECT
    position: bottom-right
    color: theme:colors.ui.tertiary

Number cascade filler ​

Fills a spare area with moving numbers instead of black. A data grid in decorative mode needs no other keys; fixed px rows keep it dense.

  • Use one small cascade on a screen at most, as a slot a readings table can take over later. Numbers that mean nothing read as noise once they fill more than a corner. Short groups of three or four rows between the blocks of a code-block column are part of that column and don't count.
  • Size the area to the rows: about font_size + 4 px per row (18 px rows at font_size: 14); smaller rows cut the text off.
  • With rows left to auto, a tall area spreads the numbers far apart. Give grid.grid-template-rows a fixed row height (about 18 px with font_size: 14) and as many rows as fit the area, and the columns a fixed width (about 44 px for four digits) so the numbers stay in tight groups.
yaml
type: custom:lcards-data-grid
data_mode: decorative
format: digit
refresh_interval: 2000
refresh_cells: 0.1
grid:
  grid-template-columns: repeat(6, 44px)
  grid-template-rows: repeat(8, 18px)
  gap: 2
style:
  font_size: 14
  color: theme:colors.ui.secondary
  align: right

Readings table ​

Dense rows of short labels with live values, for the numbers on a screen that mean something. A data grid in data mode: a code or short name, the entity's state as Home Assistant formats it, and a status word that follows the value.

  • Keep it on black, with no header box: fixed px rows about font_size + 6 px, and fixed px columns sized to the longest value. Labels sit left and values right; text_transform: uppercase makes Home Assistant's states (On, beta) read as LCARS capitals.
  • Colour by row (rows[].style.color) in one or two roles, and turn a value to the alert colour past a limit with a state map.
  • A classic data screen's upper band is a good home: three to six rows beside the sweep, in the slot a number cascade would fill.

Gauge bank ​

A row of upright gauges, as on the Picard consoles: a number cap at each end, a thin value bar and a short ruler. Good for several readings of the same kind: each is a slider with the picard-gauge-column preset, which shows the value in its top cap.

  • The bottom cap shows the entity's name; give text.name.content a code or a short label instead when the name is too long for the cap.
  • Keep the gauges narrow (about 60 px) and side by side; a bank of them sits beside a code-block column. Under about 56 px, turn the scale numbers off (style.gauge.scale.labels.enabled: false).
  • The ticks are in the entity's units (a major tick every 10, a minor every 2). For another range, such as 10 to 35 °C, set style.gauge.scale.tick_marks.major.interval and minor.interval to suit.
